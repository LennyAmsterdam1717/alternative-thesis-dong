## 3. Recurring methods, data and evaluation style (from the papers opened)

| Pattern | Where it shows up | What it means for a finance thesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Measurement theory first.** Define the construct, then check reliability and validity (content, convergent, criterion). | Nguyen & Ploeger 2025 (§3–4); Fang et al. 2025 (IRT); Su et al. 2026 (benchmarking measures against known ground truth); Du & Nguyen 2023 (validity of instability measures) | A thesis that *measures* something financial (hype, retail sentiment, management evasiveness, benchmark diversity) must validate the measure, not only use it as a feature. |
| **Controlled benchmarks with known ground truth**, simulated tier + natural tier, Spearman rank against the true ordering. | Su et al. 2026 (EmbDivBench) | Transferable design: build a controlled benchmark for financial-NLP robustness or diversity. |
| **Templated counterfactual prompts**, statistical parity, permutation tests with Bonferroni, and a **prompt-sensitivity check** (template variants, placeholder order). Finding: wording effects can exceed group effects. | Burema et al. 2026 | LLM financial-advice bias studies must vary templates; otherwise she will doubt the result. |
| **Language variation is a signal, not noise.** Separate tasks that should be robust to variation from tasks that should be sensitive to it. | Wegmann et al. 2025 (tokenization); Nguyen & Rosseel 2025; Nguyen 2025 (Compass) | Retail-investor slang, emoji and spelling are both noise (for sentiment) and signal (for community identity, hype). |
| **Data-centred analysis**: which training examples matter (training dynamics, gradients, pruning), how data composition drives robustness. | Du et al. 2025 (ACL); Du et al. 2025 (COLING); DataDivers | Financial sentiment models trained on small, homogeneous datasets (news headlines) are a natural testbed. |
| **Out-of-distribution test sets as the real evaluation**: train on one dataset, test on several others (DynaHate, AdversarialNLI, Yelp, PStance). | Herrewijnen et al. 2026; Du et al. 2025 (both) | Train on one financial register (news), test on others (StockTwits, Reddit, earnings calls). |
| **Explanations judged against humans and against other methods**, with negative findings reported (random-token regularisation ≈ human rationales; SHAP does not track attention changes). | Herrewijnen et al. 2026 | Explanations for analysts must be validated; "attention = explanation" will not convince her. |
| **CSS on social media over time, around a trigger event**: pronoun shifts (I → we), outgroup labels via embeddings + human coders + LLM check, within-group similarity, newcomer adaptation, bot check; correlational, aggregated reporting. | Sepahpour-Fard et al. 2026 (ICWSM) | Directly transferable to retail-investor communities around market events (e.g. a trading halt). |
| **Human vs LLM perception comparisons** using sociolinguistic rating methods. | Nguyen & Rosseel 2025 | Compare how investors and LLMs perceive management or retail language. |
| **Tooling from the lab**: `emb-diversity` (22 measures, semantic and style axes), STEL / style embeddings, Fightin' Words, CamemBERT/BERT fine-tuning, Botometer. | Su et al. 2026 (tool); Wegmann's style work | A thesis can reuse lab tools, which lowers the cost for her and for Frank. |

Typical models: BERT/DeBERTa-scale encoders for controlled experiments; GPT-4o-mini, Claude 3.5 Haiku and open Dutch/European LLMs (GEITje, EuroLLM) for bias audits. Typical venues: ACL/EMNLP/EACL/COLING/LREC, ICWSM, workshops (GEM, NLP+CSS). Typical datasets: social media (Twitter/X, Reddit), hate speech (CAD, DynaHate, HateXplain), GLUE-type tasks, Wikipedia.

## 6. What she would likely find exciting / unconvincing

Grounded in the papers above. This is my reading, not her stated view.

**Likely exciting**
1. **Finance as a new natural tier for her measurement agenda.** For example, measuring the diversity of financial NLP benchmarks (Financial PhraseBank, FiQA, StockTwits sets, earnings-call corpora) along semantic, stylistic and sociolinguistic dimensions with her own `emb-diversity` tool, and showing that diversity predicts robustness. This extends Su et al. 2026 and Nguyen & Ploeger 2025 beyond Wikipedia, and fits DataDivers aims (1) and (2) directly.
2. **Retail-investor communities as computational sociolinguistics**: slang, emoji, spelling ("🚀", "tendies", "HODL"), collective-identity markers (I → we), newcomer adaptation, around a trigger event. A near-copy of the Sepahpour-Fard ICWSM 2026 design, but with **market data as an external criterion** that her vaccine paper lacked.
3. **Robustness of financial sentiment models to language variation**, split into "should be robust" (semantics) vs "should be sensitive" (register, community), in the style of Wegmann et al. 2025, with OOD test sets as in Du et al. and Herrewijnen et al.
4. **A Burema-style counterfactual bias benchmark for LLM financial advice**, with template variation and permutation tests, plus a Dutch angle (she has built two Dutch benchmarks in 2026).
5. **Training-data influence and contamination as a data-centred NLP problem**: what the LLM "remembers" about firms, and whether diversity or recency of pretraining data explains look-ahead. Fits her data-centred line, provided the thesis is about *the data and the model*, not about trading profits.
6. Human-vs-LLM perception studies (Nguyen & Rosseel) applied to management language or retail posts, with a small human rating study.

**Likely unconvincing**
1. **Return-prediction leaderboards**: "our LLM feature adds 0.2 Sharpe". Nothing in her record optimises a downstream financial metric; she evaluates measures and models, not strategies.
2. **Unvalidated measures**: a "hype score" or "sentiment index" used without reliability and validity checks (her measurement-theory line, Fang et al. 2022/2025, Nguyen & Ploeger 2025).
3. **A single diversity number** (mean pairwise distance, type–token ratio), which her 2026 benchmark explicitly says not to use.
4. **Bias claims from one prompt template**, which her 2026 LREC paper shows can be dominated by wording effects.
5. **Attention weights as explanations**, which her EACL 2026 paper undercuts (random-token regularisation ≈ human rationales).
6. **Causal claims from correlational social-media data** without caveats; ethically careless scraping (she reports aggregated, de-identified results and uses API-collected data).
7. Treating language variation only as noise to be normalised away.

| Course block (topic list per public summaries) | What a thesis can build on |
| --- | --- |
| **XAI intro**: definitions (interpretability vs explanation), why XAI (debugging, fairness, GDPR, finance/banking regulation), taxonomy (intrinsic/post-hoc, local/global, model-specific/agnostic) | Framing for an analyst-facing explanation component |

| **Neural-network interpretability** (gradients, attention), with Captum and Quantus in Frank's project | Faithfulness metrics (Quantus) for financial text models |
| **Evaluating explanations**: fidelity, plausibility (needs user study), simulatability; human-level vs function-level evaluation | The human-centred evaluation design in proposals that include a small user study |
| **Fairness intro, measuring fairness** (group metrics, statistical parity, equalised odds, impossibility results), COMPAS assignments | Group-fairness metrics across firms, sectors, countries or investor profiles; Burema-style parity tests |
| **Interventions** (pre-, in- and post-processing) | Data-level interventions (reweighting, diversity-aware selection) that connect to DataDivers |

Takeaway: Frank can start the thesis with working knowledge of SHAP/LIME/Captum, faithfulness metrics, and group-fairness metrics. What he has **not** yet shown her is measurement-theory thinking (validity, reliability) and NLP-specific data analysis, so proposals should lead with those.
